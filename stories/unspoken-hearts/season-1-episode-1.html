SEASON 1 · THE FIRST GLANCE

Episode 1: The Morning Bell

Unspoken Hearts · Victor Blackthorn

Now readingSeason 1 · Episode 1
Text

The golden sunlight filtered through the trees, casting a warm glow over the grand entrance of Sapphire High School. The school stood tall, its red-bricked walls carrying years of stories within them. A soft breeze swept through the open grounds, rustling the leaves and bringing with it the faint chatter of students filling the corridors.

The air was alive with the familiar sounds of a school morning—locker doors slamming shut, hurried footsteps on the tiled floors, and the distant echoes of teachers calling out to wandering students. Groups of friends gathered in small circles, sharing jokes and last-minute assignments. For some, this was just another ordinary day. But for Aarav Sen, every morning held a quiet, unspoken anticipation.

Aarav sat in his usual spot—the third row, by the window—his notebook open but untouched. The cool morning air brushed against his face as he absentmindedly tapped his pen on the desk. He liked this seat. It gave him a perfect view of the outside world—a world that often seemed far more interesting than the equations and historical dates written on the blackboard.

The sunlight played tricks on the glass, creating a soft reflection of his own face. Neatly combed hair, deep brown eyes, and a quiet expression that rarely changed—he looked just like any other student in the room. But inside, his mind was somewhere else. Or rather, with someone else.

His gaze drifted toward the schoolyard, where a group of students stood near the basketball court. They were laughing, teasing each other, lost in their own world. And in the center of it all was her—Kavya Sharma.

Aarav’s grip on his pen tightened slightly. Kavya wasn’t just another girl in school. She was the brightest part of every morning, the reason his heart picked up pace before the first bell even rang.

She stood there, her long black hair swaying slightly with the breeze, her face lit up with laughter. Her uniform was slightly crumpled, as if she had rushed to school, and her hands were animated as she spoke. She had a way of making everything around her seem more alive—more vibrant.

Aarav had known her for years. They had been in the same class since middle school, shared group projects, sat across from each other during lunch breaks. To the world, they were just friends. And to Kavya, he was just Aarav. Someone to borrow notes from, someone to joke around with, someone… safe.

He sighed, resting his chin on his palm. If only she knew.

“Still staring at her?”

Aarav snapped out of his daze as Rohan Khanna, his best friend, slid into the seat beside him. A smirk played on Rohan’s lips as he nudged Aarav’s arm.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” Aarav muttered, looking down at his notebook.

Rohan scoffed. “Oh, come on. It’s written all over your face. Why don’t you just tell her already?”

Aarav let out a dry laugh. “And say what? ‘Hey Kavya, I know you think of me as your best friend, but I’ve been secretly in love with you for years’?”

Rohan shrugged. “Sounds better than doing nothing.”

Before Aarav could respond, the morning bell rang, and students shuffled to their seats as the teacher walked in. The moment of quiet admiration was over, replaced by the usual rhythm of school life.

As the lesson began, Aarav’s eyes flickered back to the window, just for a second. Kavya had already disappeared into the crowd, but her laughter still echoed in his mind.

Another day had begun. And so had another chapter of his silent love story.
